# Spatial Scale & Emotional Distance


## Research Question


How does physical space influence emotional relationships between visitors and historical places?


---


## Research Perspective


Historical sites are not only containers of memory. Their spatial organization, architectural scale, and visitor movement can influence how people perceive historical figures and events.


Through repeated field visits, I examined how different spatial environments create different forms of emotional distance — from solemn admiration to personal reflection.


Rather than asking whether a site feels "close" or "distant," this research explores how spatial design produces these emotional experiences.



---


# Case Study 01

## Mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang


### Monumentality and Distance


The Mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang represents one of the most significant imperial memorial landscapes in China.


The long approach toward the burial mound is lined with pine and cypress trees. Stone paths extend forward through alternating light and shadow, gradually slowing visitors' movement.

### Field Observation


I have visited the Mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang six times.


During rainy days, the site becomes quieter and more solemn. As I walked along the long pathway, my pace gradually slowed.


The emperor felt powerful yet distant, almost unreachable.


The spatial experience seemed to guide emotion step by step, allowing respect and awe to accumulate while maintaining a sense of separation.

# Case Study 02

## Tombs of Generals and Statesmen


### Different Spaces, Different Memories


Moving from imperial mausoleums to the tombs of generals and officials, the emotional atmosphere changes significantly.


Some sites use formal architecture and symbolic arrangements to emphasize historical significance, while others rely on local preservation and everyday interaction.


These differences reveal that emotional distance is not determined only by historical importance, but also by how memory is spatially presented.



---


## Huo Qubing


Near the Maoling Mausoleum, the stone sculpture:

*"Horse Treading on a Xiongnu Soldier"*

dominates the landscape.


The arrangement encourages admiration and creates a strong connection between space and official historical narrative.


Visitors encounter a carefully structured memory experience.



---


## Bai Qi


Bai Qi’s tomb presents a different atmosphere.


Compared with imperial-scale memorial spaces, the site is simpler and maintained through local attention.


Without ceremonial staging, the encounter becomes more personal.


The distance between visitor and historical figure becomes shorter.



---


## Li Si


Located in an open wheat field, Li Si’s modest tomb provides limited architectural guidance.


The surrounding landscape creates space for individual reflection on his political career and historical legacy.


Here, openness itself becomes part of the memorial experience.



---


## Lü Buwei


Near an abandoned village, Lü Buwei’s tomb is marked by scattered bricks and natural growth.


Unlike monumental memorial spaces, distance here emerges from historical neglect rather than grandeur.


Yet small offerings demonstrate that personal memory continues to exist.



---


# From Monument to Participation


Not all historical spaces create connection through scale.


Some sites develop intimacy through participation and interpretation.



## Feng Menglong Village (Suzhou)


This site combines memorial architecture with storytelling activities and cultural workshops.


Emotional connection develops through participation rather than visual monumentality.



---


## Wang Yangming Memorial Hall (Shaoxing)


At Wang Yangming Memorial Hall, engagement emerges through ideas and philosophical interpretation.


Visitors are encouraged to connect with historical figures through intellectual understanding rather than physical scale.



---


# Research Insight


Through these field observations, I found that emotional distance is not fixed by historical importance alone.


Instead, it emerges through the interaction between:

- architectural scale
- spatial organization
- visitor movement
- preservation conditions


A monumental space may create admiration while maintaining distance.


A smaller and simpler site may encourage personal reflection and emotional closeness.


Historical memory is therefore experienced not only through what a place represents, but also through how people physically encounter it.



---


# Fieldwork Reflection


Repeated visits changed my understanding of heritage spaces.


At first, I viewed monuments primarily as places that preserve historical information.


However, through longitudinal observation, I gradually realized that heritage spaces actively shape how people feel, move, and remember.


Architecture does not simply contain memory.


It participates in creating memory.
