# Narrative Structure & Emotional Orientation


## Research Question


How do museums, memorial sites, and heritage landscapes guide visitors’ understanding and emotional responses toward history?


---


# Research Perspective


Historical memory is not only preserved through physical remains.


The way history is presented also influences how visitors interpret the past.


Through field research, I examined different narrative structures used by heritage sites, including state-centered narratives, academic narratives, local narratives, and participatory narratives.


These different approaches do not simply provide information. They shape how visitors emotionally connect with historical figures, events, and places.



---


# Narrative Model 01

## State-Centered Narrative


### Guiding Collective Memory


Some heritage sites present history through a strong connection between individual figures and broader national narratives.


In these spaces, exhibitions often emphasize historical contribution, social influence, and collective memory.



---


## Zhang Qian Tomb


At the Tomb of Zhang Qian, the historical figure is presented primarily as a pioneer of the Silk Road.


The site emphasizes his role in expanding connections between different regions and civilizations.


The surrounding presentation encourages visitors to view Zhang Qian through a broader historical framework.


The emotional experience is closely connected with admiration and respect.



---


## Cao Cao Mausoleum (Gaoling)


Cao Cao represents a more complex historical figure.


Rather than presenting a simple heroic or negative image, the exhibition adopts a relatively balanced institutional narrative.


Historical materials, archaeological evidence, and explanations work together to stabilize interpretation.


Visitors are encouraged to understand complexity rather than only accept a single emotional response.



---


# Narrative Model 02

## Academic Narrative


### Understanding Before Emotion


Some heritage sites prioritize archaeological evidence and scholarly explanation.


In these spaces, emotional connection develops gradually through learning and interpretation.



---


## Erlitou Site


At the Erlitou Site, archaeological discoveries and research explanations form the central narrative.


Visitors encounter history through evidence, excavation, and academic interpretation.


The experience encourages curiosity and analytical thinking rather than immediate emotional identification.



---


## Forest of Steles Museum


The Forest of Steles Museum presents history through inscriptions, calligraphy, and textual materials.


Understanding requires careful observation and contextual knowledge.


The visitor becomes a learner first, and emotional connection emerges through intellectual engagement.



---


# Narrative Model 03

## Local Narrative


### Memory Sustained Through Community


Not all historical narratives depend on large exhibitions or official presentation.


Some memories continue through local preservation and repeated community engagement.



---


## Tomb of Zhou Yu


At Zhou Yu’s tomb in Lujiang, historical memory is maintained through local attention and continuous care.


I visited this site multiple times.


Each visit revealed restoration efforts, family documentation, and regional remembrance practices.


Unlike large memorial spaces, emotional connection develops through familiarity and continuity.



---


# Narrative Model 04

## Participatory Narrative


### Experiencing History Through Interaction


Some heritage sites create emotional connections by allowing visitors to participate in historical imagination.



---


## Chibi Site Park


At the Chibi Site Park, multimedia displays recreate the atmosphere of the Battle of Red Cliffs.


Visitors encounter history not only through reading but also through immersive experience.


The boundary between observer and participant becomes less clear.


History becomes something experienced rather than simply viewed.



---

# Research Insight


Historical interpretation is never completely separated from the way stories are constructed.


A museum label, exhibition design, spatial arrangement, or digital experience can influence how visitors approach the past.


If space shapes emotional distance, narrative shapes emotional direction.


Together, they determine how historical memory is experienced in contemporary society.



---


# Fieldwork Reflection


Through these visits, I gradually realized that heritage communication is not only about preserving historical information.


It is also about designing meaningful encounters between people and the past.


Different narratives create different possibilities for remembering.


The challenge of heritage is not only to protect historical remains, but also to consider how future generations will understand and engage with them.
